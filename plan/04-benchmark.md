# 04 — Benchmark

Package `@chowk/bench`. This is the "Proof" pillar. It must satisfy a sceptical traffic engineer and a sceptical ML engineer at once, and it must be re-runnable by a stranger in their browser.

## Rules

1. **Test seeds are touched only by the benchmark CLI and the in-browser reproduction.** Never by training, tuning or debugging. Seed ranges are in `03-controllers-and-training.md`.
2. **Every controller sees identical demand** for a given scenario and seed.
3. **Baselines are given every reasonable advantage:** Webster is told the true demand; max-pressure hysteresis is tuned on validation seeds; emergency tests include rule-based pre-emption variants.
4. **Metrics are uncensored** (delay accrues for vehicles still in the network and in the entry backlog).
5. **All results are generated by code and committed as data.** Tables in the README and the web app are rendered from that data. No number is typed by hand, by a human or an agent.
6. **Unfavourable results are published.** The headline sentence is assembled from the data whatever it says.
7. **Every figure is labelled a simulation result.**

## Pre-registered primary endpoint

Committed here before any test-seed run exists:

> **Delay per person, Morning peak scenario, Indian mixed traffic, corridor network, 20 held-out seeds: CHOWK versus area max-pressure (`mp-area`).**

This single comparison is the headline. Everything else is secondary and is described as such. No correction for multiple comparisons is applied to secondary results, and the Method page says so.

## Episode protocol

- Warm-up 300 s (not measured), then 1,800 s measured.
- Controllers act every 5 s.
- 20 test seeds per cell: 1,000,000 – 1,000,019.
- Metrics from `02-simulator.md`: delay per person, vehicle delay, stops per vehicle, throughput, travel time with completion rate, queue area, CO2, ambulance travel time, backlog, plus maximum approach wait and bus-passenger delay.

## Demand calibration

Demand is specified as a **load level** relative to capacity measured in this simulator.

1. `npm run bench:calibrate` measures saturation flow for each approach width and each vehicle mix: a standing queue discharged through a long green, after start-up loss, in vehicles per second and in road area per second. Output: `results/calibration.json`, committed.
2. For a junction, `Y = Σ over phases of q_p / s_p`. The theoretical limit under the longest allowed cycle is `Y_max = 1 − L / 150`, with `L` the lost time per cycle.
3. A scenario's **load** is `Y / Y_max` at the critical junction (J3). Load 1.0 is at theoretical capacity.

Calibration holds one through phase green and seeds an overlapping-free standing queue. The reported rates use the discharge from 6 to 46 simulated seconds over the 20 validation seeds. Curved-turn reservations can reduce service below this nominal through capacity. Custom mixes use the nearest measured mix by the sum of absolute class-share differences. Scenario loads describe the nominal peak of the input profile. Route shares determine demand reaching each approach, including upstream entries.

## Scenarios

| Id | Load | Mix | Direction split (east : west) | Cross-street share | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `morning-peak` | 0.95 | Indian mixed | 65 : 35 | 30% | Demand ramps 0.75 → 1.0 → 0.85 of nominal across the window. **Primary.** |
| `evening-peak` | 0.95 | Indian mixed | 35 : 65 | 30% | Mirror of morning |
| `off-peak` | 0.55 | Indian mixed | 50 : 50 | 35% | Checks we do no harm in light traffic |
| `festival` | 0.85 | Two-wheelers 60%, buses 8% | 50 : 50 | 40% | Scripted surges on cross streets at fixed times |
| `monsoon` | 0.95 of dry capacity | Indian mixed | 65 : 35 | 30% | Monsoon modifier; effectively over capacity |
| `oversaturated` | 1.15 | Indian mixed | 60 : 40 | 30% | Stress test; P1 |
| `cars-only` | 0.95 of its own capacity | 100% cars, lane-disciplined | 65 : 35 | 30% | The contrast case |

Turning shares **(default)**: 70% through, 15% left, 15% right on the arterial; 50/25/25 on cross streets.

## Suites

### B1. Main comparison — P0

All scenarios × {`webster`, `actuated`, `mp-count`, `mp-area`, `chowk`} × 20 seeds.

### B2. The lane-free effect — P0

`cars-only` versus `morning-peak`. For each, the relative gap between `mp-count` and `mp-area`, and between `chowk` and each baseline. The question: do count-based methods lose ground when traffic stops being lane-disciplined? The 2025 paper (arXiv 2509.21745) reports a larger learned-versus-Webster gain on laneless traffic; this suite tests whether that holds here, against a stronger baseline. Report whichever way it comes out.

### B3. Ablations — P1

`morning-peak` and `festival` × {`chowk`, `chowk-vehicle`, `chowk-count`, `chowk-pure`, `chowk-solo`} × 20 seeds. Additional columns: bus-passenger delay and car-occupant delay separately, to show what the person-weighted objective does.

### B4. Generalisation — P1

- **Load sweep:** load from 0.5 to 1.2 in steps of 0.1 on the corridor; plot delay per person against load for each controller.
- **Unseen mix:** two-wheelers 70%; and a bus-heavy mix (buses 15%).
- **Held-out network:** `grid-holdout`, never seen in training, same controllers.

### B5. Robustness — P1

`morning-peak` with: sensor noise 10%, 20%, 30%; a sensor freeze at J3 for 5 minutes; a blocked lane on the arterial for 10 minutes; a surge on a minor cross street. Report the degradation relative to the clean run for each controller.

### B6. Emergency response — P0

`morning-peak` with one ambulance every 5 minutes, end to end along the arterial. Controllers: `webster`, `mp-area`, `mp-area+preempt`, `chowk`. Report ambulance travel time **and** delay per person for everyone else, so the cost of priority is visible.

### B7. Fairness — P1

Maximum and 95th-percentile approach wait per controller; share of time any approach waited over 120 s. Guards against a controller that wins on the average by starving side streets.

### B8. Engineering — P0

Not traffic results; system measurements, reported on the Results page and in the README.

| Measurement | How |
|---|---|
| Simulator speed | × real time at 500, 1,500, 3,000 vehicles, single core, Node |
| Policy inference | Microseconds per decision |
| Cross-engine determinism | State hash after 10,000 ticks in Node, Chromium, Firefox, WebKit: identical or not |
| Frame rate | Median and 1% low at each quality tier on the development machine, hardware stated |
| Load | Transfer size and time to interactive from a cold cache |
| Tests | Count and pass status |

## Statistics

- All comparisons are **paired by seed**.
- For each cell report the mean and a **95% bootstrap confidence interval** (10,000 resamples, seeded so it is reproducible).
- For each comparison report the relative difference in means with a 95% bootstrap interval from paired resampling, and the **win rate** (share of seeds where the first controller is better).
- A difference is described as an improvement only if its interval excludes zero. Otherwise it is described as "no clear difference".
- Report medians alongside means where distributions are skewed (oversaturated scenarios).

## Output schema

`results/benchmark.json`:

```ts
interface BenchmarkFile {
  meta: {
    gitCommit: string;
    simVersion: string;          // hash of @chowk/sim sources
    policyHash: string;          // hash of the policy weights file
    createdAt: string;           // ISO date
    runtime: string;             // e.g. "node 22.x"
    wallSeconds: number;
    simulatedSeconds: number;
    seeds: number[];
  };
  runs: Array<{
    suite: string;               // 'B1' ...
    scenario: string;
    network: string;
    controller: string;
    seed: number;
    variant?: string;            // e.g. 'noise-20'
    metrics: Record<string, number>;
    finalHash: number;           // sim.hash() at the end
  }>;
  summary: Array<{              // computed, for direct display
    suite: string; scenario: string; controller: string; variant?: string;
    metric: string; mean: number; ciLow: number; ciHigh: number; median: number;
  }>;
  comparisons: Array<{
    suite: string; scenario: string; a: string; b: string; metric: string;
    relDiff: number; ciLow: number; ciHigh: number; winRate: number; clear: boolean;
  }>;
  headline: string;              // generated sentence for the primary endpoint
}
```

Also written: `results/benchmark.md` (human-readable tables) and the README results block, replaced between `<!-- results:start -->` and `<!-- results:end -->` markers by `npm run bench:readme`.

## Commands

| Command | Does |
|---|---|
| `npm run bench:calibrate` | Measures saturation flows, writes `results/calibration.json` |
| `npm run bench -- --suite B1` | Runs a suite across worker threads, appends to `results/benchmark.json` |
| `npm run bench:all` | Runs every suite |
| `npm run bench:summarise` | Recomputes summary, comparisons and headline |
| `npm run bench:readme` | Regenerates the README results block and `results/benchmark.md` |
| `npm run bench:smoke` | One seed, short episode, validation seeds; used in CI |

The CLI refuses to write results if the working tree has uncommitted changes to `packages/sim`, `packages/controllers` or the policy file, so published numbers always correspond to a commit.

## Headline sentence

Generated by `bench:summarise` from the primary endpoint:

- Clear improvement: "On 20 held-out seeds, CHOWK reduces delay per person by X% versus area max-pressure (95% CI A% to B%) and by Y% versus Webster fixed-time, in simulated Indian mixed traffic."
- No clear difference: "On 20 held-out seeds, CHOWK's delay per person is within noise of area max-pressure (difference X%, 95% CI A% to B%), and Y% below Webster fixed-time, in simulated Indian mixed traffic."
- Worse: stated just as plainly.

## In-browser reproduction ("Run it yourself")

- Runs the **quick suite**: `morning-peak` × {`webster`, `mp-area`, `chowk`} × the first 3 test seeds, full-length episodes, in a pool of Web Workers sized to the device.
- Shows progress per run, then a table of the visitor's results beside the published ones.
- Compares each run's `finalHash` and metrics with the published record. Exact match shows a tick and the line "Your browser reproduced our published numbers exactly." Any mismatch is shown in full, with the simulator version on both sides.
- Target: under 90 seconds on a four-core laptop. If the device is slow, show results as they arrive and let the visitor stop early.
- The app's bundled `simVersion` must equal the one in `benchmark.json`; a build-time check fails the build if not.

## What goes on the Results page

In this order: headline sentence; main table (B1); delay chart by scenario; lane-free effect (B2); emergency (B6); "Run it yourself"; then ablations, generalisation, robustness, fairness as collapsible sections; engineering measurements; provenance.

## What goes in the README

The headline sentence, a compact B1 table for the primary scenario, the B2 chart as an image, the B8 numbers, and a link to the full Results page. All generated.
